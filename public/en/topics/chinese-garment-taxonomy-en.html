A Prompt Engineer's Taxonomy of Chinese Garments: 12 Forms, Exact English Equivalents

FitSaga AI Reference · 2026-11-18
Hanfu, qipao, mamian, ruqun, beizi — the words get blurred together in most prompt guides. This taxonomy maps 12 Chinese garment forms to precise English prompt equivalents, with the cultural rules models need to be told explicitly.

Why a taxonomy is needed

'Hanfu' alone is a category error in prompting — it is like asking for 'Western clothing'. A model given that word produces a costume-drama average of every dynasty. The fix is a taxonomy: dynasty → garment form → detail word.

The 12 forms

1. Mamian skirt (Ming) — horse-face skirt, flat front panel, pleated sides

2. Ruqun (Tang) — chest-high ruqun, high-waisted, flowing sleeves

3. Cross-collar upper (universal) — cross-collar, right lapel over left

4. Beizi (Song) — song dynasty beizi jacket, open front, wide sleeves

5. Qipao/Cheongsam (modern) — mandarin collar qipao, fitted silk, side slit

6. Zhan'ao (Ming) — standing collar robe, Ming formal

7. Daxiushan (Tang) — large-sleeve shirt, Tang court style

8. Shenyi (Han) — cross-collar robe, wrapped, ancient Chinese

9. Hufu (Tang) — Tang nomadic-influenced outfit, fitted trousers

10. Aoqun (Ming) — jacket and skirt set, short jacket

11. Shawl (Pibo, Tang) — long silk ribbon shawl, Tang

12. Festival robe (modern) — neo-Chinese festive robe, red and gold

The two rules models must be told

Right lapel over left (left-over-right is historically for the deceased). Dynasty consistency — never mix two dynasty words in one prompt; the model will average the silhouettes into something ahistorical.

Complete formula example

a woman in a Ming dynasty mamian skirt, flat front panel with golden brocade, cross-collar right lapel over left top, buyao hairpin, classical garden, soft window light

Why glosses matter

The taxonomy works because each form has a one-to-one English gloss. 'Mamian skirt' is unusable alone; 'horse-face skirt with flat front panel and pleated sides' is fully specified. The rule: a form is only promptable when its defining visual feature is named. This is why 'hanfu' fails (no defining feature) and 'mamian' succeeds (flat panel is defining).

The translation hierarchy

Three levels of translation: sound (mamian), literal (horse-face), functional (Ming dynasty pleated skirt). For AI prompts, use all three — the sound word for specificity, the literal for concept grounding, the functional for the model's latent space. Our tested formula: mamian skirt (horse-face skirt), Ming dynasty pleated skirt, flat front panel.
