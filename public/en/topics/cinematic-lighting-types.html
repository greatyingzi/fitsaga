What cinematic lighting actually means

Cinematic lighting is not a filter — it is intentional control of direction, contrast, and color temperature. Six classic setups cover 90% of film looks.

The six setups

1. Rembrandt — 45° key light, triangle on cheek. Prompt: rembrandt lighting, soft window light from left, triangle of light on cheek

2. Butterfly / Paramount — light above, shadow under nose. Prompt: butterfly lighting, key light from above, vintage glamour

3. Split — half face lit, half in shadow. Prompt: split lighting, dramatic contrast, one side of face in shadow

4. Rim / Backlight — light behind subject. Prompt: rim lighting, glowing hair, dark background, backlit portrait

5. Golden hour — low warm sun. Prompt: golden hour, warm rim light, long soft shadows

6. Low key / Noir — minimal light, deep shadows. Prompt: low key lighting, single lamp, film noir, deep shadows

How to choose

Portrait → Rembrandt or butterfly. Drama → split or noir. Atmosphere → rim or golden hour. Your prompt should name the setup, not just say 'cinematic'.
